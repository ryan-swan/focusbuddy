# PlexiDesk Review, July 2026 (v2.5.155)

Status update, 2026-07-03: the Plexi3.0 branch implements the week-one batch and several mid-tier items from this review. Shipped there: document trash (UX finding 1), the window.prompt sweep (UX 2), quick-create completion (UX 5), the global shortcuts overlay (UX 9), onboarding and settings restructure (UX 8 and 10), OrgSwitcher accessibility (UX 11), the AI icon rename (UX 12), the calendar empty state (UX 13), hover-only control focus reveal (UX 7, partial: reveal-on-focus, not always-visible), local search categories for calendar, meetings and sign requests (functional finding 3, partial: mail and chat still need infrastructure), document version history (functional finding 7), and progressive rendering for the task list (scale flag). See docs/PLEXI-3.0-BRANCH.md for how each works and the ranked list of what remains.

This is a six-reviewer audit of the app as it stands at v2.5.155, run on 2026-07-03. Two reviewers audited the source directly, one for functional gaps and one for UI/UX, and four buyer personas judged the whole product (power user, team lead, SMB owner, late-career professional). Every code claim below was verified by grep or file read against the current tree; nothing was carried forward from older docs without re-checking. Runtime behavior was not exercised, so severity ordering on a few UX items is inferred from code rather than observed.

## The verdict in one paragraph

The single-player product is strong and the personas felt it. The Cmd+K palette, the AI proposal-apply chain, the office editors and the delete-confirmation discipline all read as real engineering. What blocks adoption is the multiplayer and multi-device story. Sync covers only nodes and widgets, cross-member org sync is not built, there is no web or mobile client and no push, and the org-scoping fixes landing in the days before this build tell a buyer the multi-user plumbing is young. Two personas called those dealbreakers independently. Everything else on this list is polish by comparison.

## Functional gaps, ranked

1. Multi-device sync covers only nodes and widgets. Documents, tables, files, knowledge and calendar do not follow the user to a second device (src/main/db/workspaceSync.ts:10, `type SyncTable = 'nodes' | 'widgets'`). Extending the existing needs_sync/rev delta contract to those tables is also the prerequisite for cross-member org sync, which is the number one adoption blocker named by the team-lead and SMB personas.
2. No web or mobile client and no push notifications. notify.ts fires desktop notifications only while the app runs and is unfocused. Every persona who manages other people called the phone gap a dealbreaker on its own. Sequenced after the durable sync layer, as the prior roadmap already intended.
3. Global search excludes Mail, Chat and Calendar, and Mail has no search of its own at all (searchAll covers nodes, widgets, documents, table rows, tables, files and knowledge only, src/main/db/search.ts:28-181; nothing in src/main/mail/). High leverage because the index, view and palette already exist.
4. Mail is single-account, password-only IMAP with no OAuth and no new-mail notification (src/main/mail/mailAccount.ts:8-10 says OAuth "can layer on later"). Gmail/Microsoft OAuth, multi-account and a notify hook are the asks. The power-user persona also wants keyboard-only triage and local full-text mail indexing before this competes on speed.
5. Calls and meetings are STUN-only (stores/call.ts:17), so they fail on symmetric NAT. Deploy coturn or a managed TURN and add it to ICE_SERVERS.
6. AI actions stop at the canvas and tasks. The ActionProposal union has 19 kinds, all widget/task/table/document-create (src/shared/types.ts:384-575). No edit-document, set-cell, send-mail, schedule-event or post-chat. The approve-then-apply chain is built; only the vocabulary is narrow.
7. Documents have no version history. Desks have canvas_snapshots time travel but documents.ts has no revision code. Reuse the snapshot pattern with a doc_snapshots table and a history panel.
8. Chat lacks @mentions and message search, and the conversation model is DM-centric rather than channel-first with membership and @here semantics, which the team-lead persona flagged as a Slack-parity requirement.
9. Calendar has no recurring events and no reminders (src/main/db/timeBlocks.ts). The recurrence math already exists in shared/schedule.ts for reports and flows. Separately, the Google Calendar tile under Connected Apps is a bookmark to calendar.google.com, not a sync, and the SMB persona read that as a fake integration. Real two-way calendar sync stays blocked on the Google OAuth credentials.
10. The notification center is partial by design. InboxView covers chat, shares and contact requests only (InboxView.tsx:13-15); sign requests, meeting invites, comments and mail are not aggregated with read state anywhere.
11. Per-seat billing is still hardcoded to quantity 1 (signal server billing.ts:108,171) even though orgs and members exist.
12. Accessibility remains thin overall, around 220 aria matches across 455 renderer files, and there is no i18n framework. Do the accessibility pass on the shared plexi primitives first so every surface inherits it; defer i18n.

## UI and UX findings, ranked

1. "Move to trash" in every document editor is a permanent hard delete. All five menu bars label it trash (DocMenuBar.tsx:109-113 and the sheet, slides, draw and design equivalents) but the chain ends in a raw `DELETE FROM documents` (src/main/db/documents.ts:217-224) with no trash table and no undo entry, while DocumentsView.tsx:211 honestly says "This cannot be undone" for the same operation. This is a no-fakery violation. Either build document soft-delete or relabel to "Delete permanently" today and route it through action history.
2. Fourteen live window.prompt call sites in flows Electron cannot service, so Rename document/sheet/drawing/deck/design, Insert link, map edge labels, sidebar URL entry, template naming and StreamDeck import silently do nothing. The repo's own e2e comments state prompt is unsupported and two widgets were already fixed for exactly this. Ship one shared inline-input dialog primitive and sweep all fourteen sites.
3. Undo coverage is uneven. Nodes and widgets get full undo, but vault entries, documents, connected apps, dashboard reset and StreamDeck reset rely on a native confirm alone. Route deletes of first-class objects through the same action history that powers UndoToast. The Boomer persona also asked for a longer undo window or a visible recently-deleted list rather than a seven-second toast.
4. Hand-rolled modals lack dialog semantics. Of 66 fixed-inset-0 overlay components, 7 declare role="dialog", 6 set aria-modal, none trap focus, and 27 never handle Escape. One shared Modal primitive in components/plexi (role, aria-modal, focus trap, Escape, initial focus, plus a styled confirm variant to replace the 20+ native confirm dialogs) fixes findings 4 and 11 together.
5. The P0 global quick-create shipped a third of itself. Consumer plumbing exists for six modules but CommandCenter.tsx:383-386 registers only New plan, New flow and Start a meeting; reports, forms and build wiring is dead and there is no New document, New sign request or Compose mail in the palette.
6. Token adoption is inverted. 210 of 251 component files hardcode stone-* (TableWidget 215 uses, MindMapWidget 173, NewNodeDialog 164, SettingsPanel 156, MailView 138) and globals.css patches over them with attribute-selector hacks. Convert the top ten stone-heavy files and forbid new stone-* in review.
7. Hover-only controls persist in 23 files (opacity-0 group-hover:opacity-100 on sidebar row actions and 21 other components), invisible to touch, keyboard and low-vision users. focus-within:opacity-100 is a one-line start.
8. Onboarding has not kept up. The first-run tour lists six tiles and omits Chat, People Map, Meet, Sign, Reports, Forms and the org switcher (FirstRunOnboarding.tsx:227-233). Separately, the Boomer persona would abandon at the Anthropic API key step in first-run; either a plain skip that does not feel punitive, or a centrally provisioned key path, and ideally a zero-setup AI tier so the AI pitch is provable in the first five minutes.
9. No global shortcuts reference and no rebinding. A cheat sheet exists only inside the document editors under Cmd+/; the global Cmd+K vs Cmd+Shift+K pair and the single-letter canvas quick-add shortcuts are documented nowhere and cannot be remapped. The power-user persona called this the cheapest high-leverage ship on the list since the palette already knows every command.
10. The settings panel mixes user settings with developer surfaces (model routing tables, tier forcing, NSHapticFeedbackPerformer badges) in 9 to 11 pixel low-contrast type. Split into a plain page (theme, text size, account, backup) with an Advanced section collapsed by default, and raise the type size.
11. OrgSwitcher is mouse-only with no Escape, no aria state and no arrow-key navigation, thin for the control that swaps the entire workspace.
12. Nine components still use the auto_fix_high AI icon after the standardisation on auto_awesome. Mechanical rename.
13. CalendarView is the one sampled major view with no empty, loading or error state. One quiet empty-month hint line is enough.
14. No visible Help or support entry point anywhere in the app. Every persona over 45 looked for one. A question-mark menu item linking the existing help pages costs little.

There is also one scale flag from the power-user pass. AllTasksView renders its full filtered list with a plain .map() and no virtualization exists anywhere in the renderer, which will degrade after months of real data. Worth a windowing pass on the big lists before it is felt.

## Highest-leverage new-feature opportunities

Ranked by value per unit of new code, given what already exists. Suite-wide search across mail, chat and calendar, because the index and palette are built. AI-acts-everywhere by widening the ActionProposal vocabulary, because the approve-and-apply chain is built. Document version history, because the snapshot pattern is proven on desks. A unified notification center, because InboxView and the socket events are there. Mail OAuth and multi-account, because the account record was designed for it. A global shortcuts overlay with rebindable keys, because the palette already knows every command. A Raycast-style scripting layer on the existing local REST API is the sleeper opportunity for power users.

## Persona verdicts

The power user scored it 7/10, would trial it as a daily driver today on the strength of the palette and the AI proposal chain, and holds evangelism behind the shortcuts system and proof of performance at scale. The team lead would not move a distributed team on until cross-member sync and mobile push exist, and called everything else secondary to those two. The SMB owner scored it 4/10 as a full Microsoft/Google replacement and 7/10 as a pilot alongside it, with hosted-or-synced mail plus mobile, sync trust, and an admin/SSO story as the three independent dealbreakers, and asked for a quiet quarter of zero scoping bugs before trusting the sync layer. The late-career professional praised the document editor, the delete confirmations and the text-size control as genuine craft, and would still walk at the API-key onboarding step and the developer-flavoured settings panel.

## Suggested sequence

Week-one fixes that cost little and remove active harm are the trash mislabel, the fourteen dead prompt sites, the quick-create completion, the icon rename, the calendar empty state and a Help entry point. The strategic block is the sync ladder, extending workspaceSync to all tables, then cross-member org sync, then web/mobile with push, in that order, since three of four personas anchor adoption on it. In parallel, the shared Modal primitive plus the token conversion of the top ten files carries the design-system rollout, and suite-wide search plus the AI vocabulary widening are the two cheapest visible wins.
